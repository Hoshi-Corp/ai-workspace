# Restaurant Curator

Run once a week, every Thursday.

Curate noteworthy restaurants in Ottawa as the primary focus, with a secondary focus on worthwhile food destinations in cities along the Toronto–Montreal corridor.

Prioritize cuisines, regional specialties, techniques, and dining experiences outside the regular North American menu. Favor genuine regional depth, culturally specific dining traditions, unusual preparations or ingredients, traditional techniques, communal dining experiences, tasting menus, fermentation, live-fire cooking, and other distinctive experiences where the food or way of eating provides insight into a particular culture.

Use experiences like Filipino Kamayan as the benchmark for memorable, culturally distinctive meals: the format and traditions of the meal should contribute to the experience, not just the novelty of the food.

Deprioritize or skip places whose main draw is pizza, tacos, burgers, fish and chips, generic pub fare, or similar standard fare. Do not recommend a restaurant simply because it serves “international” or “Asian” food; look for regional specificity and depth rather than generic novelty.

Surface genuinely interesting current openings, established hidden gems, destination-worthy restaurants, unusual regional cuisines, tasting experiences, and culturally specific meals. Include temporary events only when the food itself is genuinely distinctive, not merely because many vendors or cuisines are gathered in one place.

For each recommendation, briefly explain what makes it worth visiting and what specifically to order or experience. Identify particular dishes, regional specialties, dining rituals, or preparations that distinguish it whenever possible.

Use current information to verify that recommended restaurants are still operating, that suggested dishes or experiences are currently available when possible, and that new openings or special events are actually current. Include source links supporting these details; clearly identify any uncertainty rather than presenting unverified information as fact.

Do not recommend the Ottawa Chinatown Night Market as a food discovery. The user has visited several times and finds its offerings repetitive, greasy, and novelty-oriented rather than culturally distinctive.

Keep the report selective. Favor a few well-supported, genuinely worthwhile recommendations over a long list. Do not pad the report with ordinary options when nothing meets this standard.
